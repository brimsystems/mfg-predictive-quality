Section 1

Executive Summary

The cell's two monitoring units already catch the largest share of what the cavity pressure curve can tell: their template alarms and sort cover 47.6% of confirmed defects. Extending that SPC to every shot, with control-chart rules 1 and 2 (+8.2 points) and drift detection (+14.4 points), brings coverage to 70.2% without any machine learning. On top of that, virtual metrology puts a predicted weight and critical dimension on every shot, and adds +0.0 points; the anomaly model flagged 3 of 3 novel events in the test period and adds +4.0 points. A supervised defect model was tested and not deployed, because the defects it can learn from were mostly found by the template's own sort. 25.8% of confirmed defects left no trace in the curve. The added layers raise 3.3 alarms per shift, inside the budget of three to six; with the template's own alarms, 5.6.

Section 2

The Cell and How Quality Is Controlled

Two 200-ton presses, IM-11 and IM-12, run six instrumented molds in runs of two to six days. Each mold carries cavity pressure sensors in one or two of its cavities. A post-gate sensor sits just inside the gate and sees the melt first; an end-of-fill sensor sits at the far end of the flow path and sees the cavity fill last. The other cavities are not sensed and follow the sensed one at a stable offset.

2.1

One shot's curve

Each shot produces one pressure curve per sensor. The template is the curve of the approved process, taken at the first-shot approval of each run. The monitoring unit reduces every curve to a few summary values: the fill integral (area under the curve while the cavity fills), the pack integral (area while hold pressure packs the part, up to gate seal), the peak pressure, the gate seal time (when the gate freezes and pressure starts to fall), the end-of-fill pressure (at the end-of-fill sensor when the cavity is full) and the cycle integral (the whole area).

A good M-2118 shot against its template

The shot tracks its template through fill, transfer and pack; each summary value is read from a fixed part of the curve.

2.2

The monitoring unit, the sort and the audits

The unit compares each summary value with the template. Each value has a warning band and a wider alarm band around the template value, set from the launch experiment; a deviation is stated in bands, where 1 band is the distance from the template value to the alarm limit. A shot outside any alarm band is an alarm, and the press robot drops it in the reject bin: the sort. An inspector reviews the bin every shift (the reject-bin review), confirms the defective pieces and returns the good ones. Separately, quality pulls an hourly audit of 6 to 20 pieces, measures weight and the critical dimension, checks them visually, and plots the mean weight on audit control charts with the Western Electric rules 1, 2, 4 and 5.

2.3

How outcomes are known

Four records say which pieces were defective. Sort dispositions (the reject-bin review) and audit measurements are tied to a specific shot: the robot records the cycle it sampled on 88% of audit pieces. Packing tallies record defects by job, hour and code with no shot, and customer returns arrive weeks later. A job's quality is taken as final 21 days after its last shot. Linkage coverage, the share of confirmed defective pieces tied to a shot, is 27.6%.

How catch is measured

  • Shot-level catch: on defects linked to a shot, whether the layer alarmed on that shot.
  • Job-hour coverage: on all confirmed defects (sort review, audits and packing tallies), whether the layer alarmed on that job in that hour or shortly before (two hours; eight for drift, whose signal stays open until its reset). Each defect is credited to the layer that alarmed first in time.
  • Points means percentage points of confirmed defects under job-hour coverage. Alarms per shift counts the job-hours in which a layer fired, per eight hours of production.
  • SPC layers use the shop's existing statistical process control; ML layers use models trained on the data. Test period: December 2025 to March 2026, after training through September and validation in October and November.
Section 3

What the Existing Process Control Caught

SPC The template alarms and sort cover 47.6% of confirmed defects (job-hour coverage) at 2.21 alarm hours per shift. At the shot level, 21.2% of audit-found defects (pieces pulled on a clock, independent of the sort) fell on shots the template had alarmed. Every sort-found defect is caught by construction.

Over the fifteen months the units sorted 1.27% of production shots. The review found 31% of sorted shots good: 82,530 good pieces in the reject bin, $71,504 at standard cost.

Section 4

What Extending SPC Added, Without Machine Learning

Nothing in this section is machine learning. It runs the shop's own control-chart rules on every shot instead of the hourly audit sample, and adds two standard drift statistics.

4.1

Charting every shot

SPC With limits from the first 500 shots after each approval, rule 1 (one point beyond 3 sigma) fires on 8.5% of points, rule 2 (two of three beyond 2 sigma) on 19.6%, rule 4 (four of five beyond 1 sigma) on 41.6% and rule 5 (eight in a row on one side) on 74.5%. Consecutive shots are correlated: the melt, the mold and the material change slowly, so one shot looks much like the last. Patterns that are rare in an hourly sample are normal from shot to shot, and the run rules (4 and 5) read them as shifts. Rules 1 and 2 are kept: they add +8.2 points at 0.96 alarms per shift.

4.2

Drift detection

SPC An EWMA (exponentially weighted moving average) smooths each shot's fill and pack integral so a slow shift stands out from shot-to-shot noise. A CUSUM (cumulative sum) adds up small deviations in one direction until they cross a limit, and here runs on end-of-fill pressure (vents), on pack-integral variance (check ring) and on gate seal time (mold temperature). Each resets at an approval, a documented correction, a vent cleaning, PM, a ring replacement or a lot change.

Drift detection adds +14.4 points at 0.60 alarms per shift. Where a drift signal and a template alarm both fired on a job, the drift signal came first by a median 134 shots (about 1.5 hours) across 122 cases in the test period. The vent CUSUM leads the rise in burn tallies by a median 8,885 shots (about 80 hours of production).

Vent restriction on job J-250128 (M-2119)

End-of-fill pressure falls as the vents load, and the CUSUM signals before burns start to climb at packing (about 2 hours before the first hour with three or more burns).

The same statistics carry two maintenance signals. Check-ring leakage on IM-12 shows as rising pack-integral variance until the ring was replaced, with no change in the mean. Resin lot changes step the fill integral at the load: the lot-step test flagged 49 of 78 lot changes that fell inside a job.

4.3

SPC subtotal

SPC The template, rules 1 and 2 and drift detection together cover 70.2% of confirmed defects. The two added SPC layers raise 1.56 alarms per shift on top of the template's 2.21.

Section 5

What Machine Learning Added

Two model components were deployed. Each section says what the model outputs, how it is computed, how it was checked, and what it enables.

5.1

Virtual metrology

ML Output. A predicted part weight and critical dimension for every shot, for each cavity; the unit screen shows the mean over the active cavities. How it is computed. A gradient-boosted regression trained on audited pieces linked to their shot (186,769 pieces), using that shot's summary values normalized to the template, the press's machine-side values and the context (lot, dryer, maintenance counters, setpoints). It estimates the parts just made; it does not forecast future shots.

How it was checked. On the test period the error is 1.36 times the gauge R&R on weight and 1.76 times on the dimension, with R² of 0.63 and 0.85 (medians over mold and press, five seeds; calibration slopes 1.02 and 1.06). Weight R² is lower because a shot moves weight by only a few tenths of a percent, close to the scale's own error, so the gauge noise is a larger share of what is measured. Without the cavity pressure values the dimension error rises to 3.38 times gauge.

Predictions follow the gauge closely across the tolerance band; the spread is mostly the gauge's own error.

Summer backtest. The test period (December to March) carried little mold-temperature drift, which is part of why virtual metrology added no catch there. A backtest trained on January to May and evaluated on June to September, when mold temperatures drifted with the season, gives an error of 1.48 times gauge on weight and 2.56 times on the dimension (R² 0.58 and 0.70), and adds +0.0 points in that window (0.4 points of dimensional defects).

Why it adds little catch. 85% of dimensional defects in the test period fell on shots the template had already alarmed, because the pack excursions that push a dimension out of tolerance also leave the pack band. The value of virtual metrology is a measurement on every shot, not more catches.

What it enables.

  • Dimensional drift visible between audits, shot by shot, instead of at the next hourly sample.
  • A longer audit interval on molds where the error sits within 1.5 times the gauge error on weight and 1.8 times on the dimension on both presses: M-2260, M-2301.
  • A predicted dimension for every part in the customer lot record.
  • A prediction-triggered audit: an extra sample pulled when the predicted dimension passes 75% of tolerance (0.16 per shift on the test period). It is advisory and never sorts a part.
5.2

Anomaly detection

ML Output. An anomaly score for every shot, and an unusual state when two of the last ten shots score above the threshold. How it is computed. An isolation forest per mold and press, trained only on validated good shots (after approval, no alarm, no linked defect), with no defect labels. It scores how far each shot's values have moved from the run's own recent shots, so a run sitting steadily off its template is left to the template and the charts.

How it was checked. It flagged 3 of 3 novel events in the test period (events no rule was written for: nozzle drool, a failing heater zone). 81% of its flags fall on shots the template passed. It adds +4.0 points at 1.71 alarms per shift: 2.4 points per alarm per shift, against 8.5 for rules 1 and 2 and 24.0 for drift detection.

Section 6

Where It Fell Short

6.1

Supervised defect model: tested and not deployed

A gradient-boosted classifier trained on confirmed defects linked to a shot catches 83.0% of defective shots against the template's 80.3% at the template's own alarm rate. Most of those labels exist because the template sorted the shot and the reviewer confirmed it, so the model is largely learning to reproduce the template. Scored only on audit-found defects, pieces the robot pulled on a clock and independent of the template (512 defective of 9,500 audited shots in the test period), it catches 8.8% against the template's 7.4% at the template's alarm rate on those shots (1.1%).

On all shot-linked labels the model tracks the template; on defects found independently of the sort both catch far less, and the model's edge is small.

6.2

Caught by none

25.8% of confirmed defects had no alarm from any layer. Most are handling damage, contamination, black specks and splay that leave no trace in the cavity pressure curve; no layer built on the curve can see them.

6.3

Anomaly threshold

The threshold was set so 0.45% of shots on the fit window are flagged. On the test period 4.0% of production shots were flagged, 0.49% of validated good shots; the two-in-ten rule turns those flags into 1.71 alarms per shift.

6.4

Curve autoencoder

An autoencoder on the retained curves was run as a comparison. It flagged 2.9% of novel-event curves against 7.3% of other retained curves, so it did not separate the events and was not deployed.

6.5

Linkage

Only 27.6% of confirmed defective pieces are tied to a shot; packing tallies and returns carry a job and hour at most. That is why job-hour coverage is the main measure, and why it credits a layer for alarming near a defect rather than on the defective shot itself.

7.1

Other limitations

  • The layer comparison credits a layer for alarming on the job near a defect; it does not prove the alarm would have prevented that defect.
  • Returns arrive weeks after the job; a job's figures are final only 21 days after its last shot.
  • Virtual metrology is trained on the six molds and two presses here; a new mold needs its own audit history before its predictions are used.
