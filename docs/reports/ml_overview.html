What the units already caught

The template alarms and the sort remove a real share of defects and cost the shop false rejects in return. Over the fifteen months the units sorted 1.27% of production shots; the reject-bin review found 31% of those shots good, and the sorted shots held 20% of all defective pieces. The rest of the defects either carried no curve signal or sat inside the bands.

The units' recorded alarm states agree with the platform's recomputation from the summary values and templates on 99.96% of production shots, so the layers below build on the same alarm logic the technicians see.

What charting every shot added

Running the audit chart's rules on every shot floods the technician unless the rule set is cut down. With limits from the first 500 shots after approval, rule 5 (eight in a row on one side) fires on 75% of points and rule 4 on 42%: cavity pressure values drift slowly and are correlated from shot to shot, which those rules read as a shift. Rules 1 and 2 alone raise 0.96 alarms per shift and add 8.3 points of confirmed defects to what the template caught.

Drift detection and the maintenance signals

Drift detection warns early because the slow mechanisms move the process for hours before parts go bad. EWMA on the pack and fill integrals and CUSUMs on end-of-fill pressure, pack-integral variance and gate seal time raise 0.60 signal onsets per shift and add 5.6 points. The chart below follows one job on M-2043: end-of-fill pressure declines as the vents load, the CUSUM signals, and the burns tallied at packing follow about 0 hours later.

The same layer carries the maintenance signals: the check ring's leakage shows as pack-integral variance on IM-12 until the ring was replaced, and resin lot changes step the fill integral at the load (lot-step tests flag 49 of 78 lot changes inside a job).

The per-shot measurement

Virtual metrology works because its labels are abundant: 186,769 audited pieces, 88% of them linked to their shot by the robot's recorded cycle. On the test period the pooled model predicts part weight within 1.36 times the gauge R&R and the critical dimension within 1.76 times, with R² of 0.63 and 0.85 (medians over mold and press, five seeds).

Test-period audit pieces, pooled model. Dashed lines are the drawing tolerance.

The value is between audits: a predicted weight and dimension on every shot instead of one sample an hour. As an alarm it added 0.0 points on dimensional codes in the test period, because the winter months carried little mold-temperature drift and 89% of the dimensional defects fell on shots the template had already alarmed on. Its use there is the audit plan: where the prediction sits within about 1.5 times the gauge error, the shop could stretch the audit interval on that mold (per mold in the technical report). Without the cavity pressure values the dimension error rises to 3.4 times gauge, which is the in-mold signal's value in one number.

The supervised defect model

A model trained on confirmed defects does not beat the template limits on the common codes, and this section is kept to say so. At the template's own alarm rate it catches 83.0% of confirmed defective shots against the template's 80.3%. It ranks sink and dimensional defects better than the band does, because the pack integral carries graded information the band throws away. On the rare codes the label counts are too small for anything usable.

The anomaly layer

The anomaly model covers what no rule was written for. It flagged 6 of 6 novel events (a failing check ring, a failing heater zone, nozzle drool, a wrong material), 3 of them in the test period; 81% of its flags fall on shots the template passed. With two flags in ten shots required, it raises 1.71 alarms per shift and adds 3.0 points.

The alarm budget

Thresholds are set for what one technician per shift can investigate. The deployed rule set, drift onsets and the anomaly layer together raise 3.3 alarms per shift, inside the three to six the shop can work; the sort runs on its own. Of the new layers, the control-chart rules added the most (8.3 points).

Limitations

Labels for rare codes are too few to train on. Packing tallies carry no shot, so the layer comparison works at the job-hour grain and credits a layer for alarming near a defect, not for the defect itself. 26% of confirmed defects had no alarm from any layer; most carry no curve signal (handling, contamination, specks) and stay unexplained by design. The anomaly threshold is set on the fit window (0.45%); on validated test-period shots the flag rate was 0.49%. The curve autoencoder, run as a comparison, did not separate the novel events from other retained curves; the technical report gives its numbers.
