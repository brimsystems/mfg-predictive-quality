Section 1

Executive Summary

The template sort is the cell's main detection layer and the only one that removes parts. From May 2025 to March 2026 it flagged 20.3% of defective audited pieces against 1.5% of good ones: +18.8 points above chance [+15.5, +22.0]. It detects fill-volume defects best (+49.8 points) and packing and shrinkage defects next (+19.9).

What the curve cannot see sets the limit for every layer built on it. Material defects (splay, black specks, contamination) sit at chance for the template (-0.3 points), and gate and flow-front defects close to it (+2.0 and +3.4). 75% of defective audited pieces were flagged by no layer at all.

Every other layer raises an investigation, or for virtual metrology an advisory audit, and each is set to the alarm budget and judged on its own measure:

  • Control-chart rules 1 and 2 detect +0.9 points above chance beyond the template, mostly material defects the template cannot see (+9.0 points).
  • Drift detection adds 0.0 points at the shot. Tested on the mechanisms it is built for, the fill EWMA flags 59% of resin lot changes that step the fill integral against 15% of those that do not; the vent and check-ring detectors do no better than chance.
  • Anomaly detection flagged 4 of 4 novel events in the period, 2 of them before the template; at the shot it adds +0.1 points.
  • Virtual metrology puts a measurement on every shot: dimension error 2.39 times the gauge R&R (2.89 in the warm months) and weight 1.35 times. As an advisory it adds +8.8 points on packing and shrinkage defects and +31.0 on dimensional and warp defects in the warm months.

The two model layers together add +1.3 points above chance overall [+0.6, +2.2]. The added layers raise 3.83 alarm episodes per shift, inside the budget of three to four investigations.

Section 2

The Cell and How Quality Is Controlled

Two 200-ton presses, IM-11 and IM-12, run six instrumented molds in runs of two to six days. Each mold carries cavity pressure sensors in one or two of its cavities. A post-gate sensor sits just inside the gate and sees the melt first; an end-of-fill sensor sits at the far end of the flow path and sees the cavity fill last. The other cavities are not sensed and follow the sensed one at a stable offset.

2.1

One shot's curve

Each shot produces one pressure curve per sensor. The template is the curve of the approved process, taken at the first-shot approval of each run. The monitoring unit reduces every curve to a few summary values: the fill integral (area under the curve while the cavity fills), the pack integral (area while hold pressure packs the part, up to gate seal), the peak pressure, the gate seal time (when the gate freezes and pressure starts to fall), the end-of-fill pressure (at the end-of-fill sensor when the cavity is full) and the cycle integral (the whole area).

A good M-2118 shot against its template

The shot tracks its template through fill, transfer and pack; each summary value is read from a fixed part of the curve.

2.2

The monitoring unit, the sort and the audits

The unit compares each summary value with the template. Each value has a warning band and a wider alarm band around the template value, set from the launch experiment; a deviation is stated in bands, where 1 band is the distance from the template value to the alarm limit. A shot outside any alarm band is an alarm, and the press robot drops it in the reject bin: the sort. An inspector reviews the bin (the reject-bin review), confirms the defective pieces and returns the good ones. Separately, quality pulls an hourly audit of 6 to 20 pieces, measures weight and the critical dimension and checks them visually.

2.3

How detection is measured

Audits pull pieces on a clock, independent of every layer, and 88% of audit pieces are tied to the shot they came from on all six molds. That makes them the fair test of detection: a layer is credited only for flagging the shot a defective piece came from, and is charged for the good pieces it flags in the same audits.

Definitions

  • Detected above chance: the share of defective audited pieces whose shot a layer flagged, minus the share of good audited pieces from the same audits whose shot it flagged. The good-piece share is the chance level: what flagging shots at random would score. 95% intervals come from resampling whole audits.
  • Incremental: layers are added in the order template, rules, drift, anomaly, virtual metrology; each is credited only for pieces no earlier layer flagged, minus the same share for good pieces.
  • Evaluation period: May 2025 to March 2026 for every layer. Virtual metrology and the anomaly model are retrained at the start of each month on all earlier data and applied to that month only, so every month is out of sample, including the summer of 2025.
  • Alarm episode: a layer's flagged shots on a job, with gaps under 30 shots, count as one investigation. Each layer's deployed threshold is set from this budget, and every figure in this report uses those thresholds.
  • Defect groups: fill volume (short shot, flash); packing and shrinkage (sink, void, dimensional, warp); flow front (weld line, burn); gate and other (gate vestige, other); material (splay, black specks, contamination).
Section 3

What the Template Detects

SPC The template flags 20.3% of defective audited pieces and 1.5% of good ones, +18.8 points above chance. Detection falls from fill volume down to material, as the physics says it should: a short shot or flash changes the fill and pack integrals the unit watches, while a black speck or a contamination leaves the curve untouched. Fill-volume defects are flagged on 51% of pieces; material defects on no more than the good-piece rate.

Section 4

What Extending SPC Added, Without Machine Learning

Nothing in this section is machine learning. It runs the shop's own control-chart rules on every shot instead of the hourly audit sample, and adds standard drift statistics.

4.1

Control-chart rules on every shot

SPC Consecutive shots are correlated: the melt, the mold and the material change slowly, so one shot looks much like the last. The charts therefore plot each shot against what the previous shot predicts, which leaves close to independent points for rules 1 and 2. Their deployed limits are 4.05 sigma (rule 1) and 2.70 sigma (rule 2), wider than the standard 3 and 2 sigma, and chosen to meet the alarm budget: at the standard limits the rules would raise 12.1 alarm episodes per shift, against 1.03 at the deployed limits. Rules 4 and 5 are not run on every shot.

At the deployed limits the rules detect +9.3 points above chance on their own and +0.9 beyond the template. Almost all of that is material defects (+9.0 points): wet resin moves the fill from shot to shot without leaving the template's band.

4.2

Drift detection

SPC An EWMA (exponentially weighted moving average) smooths the fill and pack integrals so a step stands out from shot-to-shot noise; a CUSUM (cumulative sum) adds up small deviations in one direction until they cross a limit, here on end-of-fill pressure (vents), on pack-integral variance (check ring) and on gate seal time (mold temperature). Each resets at an approval, a documented correction, a vent cleaning, PM, a ring replacement or a lot change. The deployed limits are the standard ones; after a detector fires, a new excursion of the same detector within 3,000 shots is the same investigation unless a reset comes between. Drift raises 0.98 episodes per shift and adds 0.0 points of shot-level detection beyond the template and rules.

A drift detector is meant to signal before defects appear, so each is also tested against the mechanism it was built for:

  • Fill EWMA at resin lot changes. It flags 59% [43%, 74%] of the 37 mid-run lot changes that step the fill integral, within a median 6 shots, against 15% [4%, 42%] of the 13 that do not. It works as a step detector.
  • End-of-fill CUSUM and vent restriction. Its first firing after a vent cleaning comes before the rise in burns on 78% of 40 cleaning intervals, against 74% with the burn rise placed at a random time. It fires about as early as chance would put it, a median 10,782 shots ahead, so it is not timed to the vents.
  • Variance CUSUM and check-ring wear. It flagged none of 16 jobs run on a worn ring before their first sink or void. Ring wear builds over months and barely moves within a job, while the detector's baseline is each job's own first 500 shots, so wear present at the start is part of the baseline.

Section 5

What Machine Learning Added

Two model components were deployed. Both are retrained at the start of each month on everything before it, so each month is scored by the model that would have been in use then.

5.1

Virtual metrology

ML Output. A predicted part weight and critical dimension for every shot and cavity. How it is computed. A gradient-boosted regression trained on audited pieces linked to their shot, using that shot's summary values normalized to the template, the press's machine-side values (including the mold temperatures and cycle time) and the context (lot, dryer, maintenance counters, setpoints). It estimates the parts just made; it does not forecast future shots.

As a measurement. Over the eleven months the dimension error is 2.39 times the gauge R&R (R² 0.71) and the weight error 1.35 times (R² 0.60), medians over mold and press. In the warm months, June to September, the dimension error rises to 2.89 times gauge against 2.14 in the other months, because summer chiller load and controller drift move the dimension through the mold temperature as well as the pack. Weight holds at 1.36 times. Calibration slopes are 1.12 (dimension) and 1.08 (weight): predictions are slightly compressed. Without the cavity pressure values the dimension error rises to 3.56 times gauge and the weight error to 1.91.

As an advisory audit. When a cavity's predicted dimension passes 75% of tolerance, an extra audit sample is requested; the advisory never sorts a part. It raises 1.09 episodes per shift and adds +8.8 points [+5.2, +13.2] on packing and shrinkage defects beyond every SPC layer, and +31.0 on dimensional and warp defects in the warm months, when the template misses parts moved by mold temperature. Overall it adds +1.2 points.

5.2

Anomaly detection

ML Output. An anomaly score for every shot, and an unusual state when two of the last ten shots score above the threshold. How it is computed. An isolation forest per mold and press, trained only on validated good shots, with no defect labels. It scores how far each shot's values have moved from the run's own recent shots, so a run sitting steadily off its template is left to the template and the charts.

Per event. 4 novel events fall in May 2025 to March 2026; the model flagged all 4, 2 before the template, with a median lead of 19 minutes over the first confirmed defect or technician action. On nozzle drool the template alarmed first. On the heater-zone failure it flagged 94 minutes into the event, 43 minutes before the template, but 60 minutes after the shop had already responded, so on that event it would not have changed what the technician did. It raises 0.73 episodes per shift.

5.3

The two models together

Beyond every SPC layer the two models add +1.3 points [+0.6, +2.2] above chance overall, and +3.4 in the warm months, almost all of it virtual metrology on packing, shrinkage and dimensional defects.

Section 6

Where It Fell Short

6.1

Supervised defect model: tested and not deployed

A gradient-boosted classifier trained on confirmed defects linked to a shot detects 31.4% of defective shots against the template's 37.4% at the template's own alarm rate. Most of those labels exist because the template sorted the shot and the reviewer confirmed it, so the model largely learns the template. Scored only on audit-found defects (456 defective of 9,554 audited shots in December to March), it detects 8.6% against the template's 7.2% at the template's alarm rate (1.3%): no material gain.

6.2

Defects the curve cannot see

75% of defective audited pieces were flagged by no layer. Material, gate and flow-front defects leave little or no trace in the cavity pressure curve; no layer built on the curve can detect them, and the shop's visual checks remain the control for them.

6.3

Drift detectors as early warnings

Only the fill EWMA does what it was built for. The vent CUSUM's first firing after a cleaning precedes the burn rise no more often than chance, and the variance CUSUM cannot see check-ring wear that is already present when a job starts. The realism requirement that the end-of-fill CUSUM lead the burn rise by 2,000 to 6,000 shots on at least 70% of vent-cleaning intervals is not met at the deployed limits: the lead falls in that range on 15% of intervals.

6.4

Anomaly detection at the shot

The anomaly model flags every novel event, but adds +0.1 points at the shot: its flags mostly fall on shots an earlier layer already flagged, and novel events are a few per year. A curve autoencoder was run as a comparison: it flagged 0.0% of retained novel-event curves against 5.1% of other retained curves.

7.1

Other limitations

  • Shot-level detection rests on audit pieces, 2,093 of them defective in the evaluation period; small groups carry wide intervals.
  • A detection credits a layer for flagging the shot a defective piece came from; it does not show that the alarm would have prevented the defect.
  • Returns arrive weeks after the job; a job's figures are final only 21 days after its last shot.
  • Virtual metrology is trained on the six molds and two presses here; a new mold needs its own audit history before its predictions are used.
